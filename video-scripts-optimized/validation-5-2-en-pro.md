## Overview

Revalidation is essential to ensure continuity of validation state after changes or over time.

What is Revalidation?

Revalidation is the re-verification of the system or process after changes or over time.

Re-verification of the system Re-verification of the process Re-verification of equipment Ensure quality continuity of Revalidation:

- Ensures continuity of validation state
- Ensures changes do not have negative effects
- Ensures regulatory compliance
- Essential for continuous quality Revalidation is essential after significant changes Revalidation is essential periodically Revalidation must be documented When is Revalidation Required?
There are several situations that require revalidation.


## Main Situations Requiring Action

 After Significant Changes: Equipment change Process change Raw material change Site change After a Specific Time Period: Periodic revalidation Usually every 1-3 years Depends on system type Depends on usage After Problems: After deviations After quality failures After complaints After recalls After Improvements: After process improvement After equipment update After software update After procedure update Types of Revalidation  There are several types of revalidation, each serving a different purpose.

- **Full Revalidation**: Re-execute all validation stages Essential after large changes Requires DQ, IQ, OQ, PQ Costly but comprehensive Partial Revalidation: Re-execute specific stages Essential after small changes Requires only affected stages Less costly and faster Periodic Revalidation: Re-validate periodically Essential to maintain validation state Based on risk assessment Usually every 1-3 years Continued Process Verification: Continuous performance monitoring
- Essential for continuous processes Requires monitoring and analysis Part of CPV Revalidation Procedures  Executing revalidation requires following specific procedures.
