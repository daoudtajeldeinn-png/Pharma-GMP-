## Overview

These agencies play a vital role in ensuring the quality and safety of pharmaceuticals.

- FDA - US Food and Drug Administration  FDA is the US Food and Drug Administration, one of the oldest and most important regulatory agencies in the world.
Established in 1906 Responsible for regulating drugs in the United States Issues GMP regulations (21 CFR Parts 210/211) Conducts inspections and monitoring FDA GMP Regulations: Part 210: Good Manufacturing Practice for drugs Part 211: Good Manufacturing Practice for finished pharmaceuticals FDA applies strict standards to: US manufacturing facilities Facilities that export to the US Companies seeking approval for new products EMA - European Medicines Agency  EMA is the European Medicines Agency, responsible for regulating drugs in the European Union.

Established in 1995 Headquarters in London (currently Amsterdam) Coordinates between national regulatory authorities in Europe Issues

- EU GMP guidelines
- EU GMP Guidelines: Volume 1: Good Manufacturing Practice for medicinal products Volume 2: Good Manufacturing Practice for starting materials Annexes: Specific guidelines (such as Annex 1 for sterile products) EMA focuses on: Harmonizing standards in Europe Evaluating marketing authorization applications Post-marketing surveillance of medicines WHO - World Health Organization  WHO is the World Health Organization, an international organization that sets GMP standards for developing countries.
Established in 1948 Headquarters in Geneva Issues international GMP guidelines Helps countries implement GMP standards

- WHO GMP Guidelines: GMP guidelines for active pharmaceutical ingredients GMP guidelines for starting materials GMP guidelines for sterile products WHO focuses on: Technical assistance to developing countries International standard harmonization Improving drug quality globally SFDA - Saudi Food and Drug Authority  SFDA is the Saudi Food and Drug Authority, responsible for regulating drugs in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
