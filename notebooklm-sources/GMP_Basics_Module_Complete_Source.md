## What is GMP?

Good Manufacturing Practices or GMP is a set of standards and practices that ensure pharmaceutical products are manufactured safely and effectively.

GMP stands for Good Manufacturing Practice.

GMP is a quality assurance system

- Ensures products are manufactured consistently with quality control Minimizes risks of manufacturing errors
- Ensures patient safety and consumer protection GMP covers all aspects of manufacturing - from raw materials to the final product.
- **It covers**: Facility design Employee training Document management Process control Quality monitoring History of GMP  The history of GMP dates back to the mid-20th century, but its development was gradual over the years.
- **Key 1962**: The US FDA published the first GMP regulations after the thalidomide scandal 1970s: Development of GMP regulations in Europe 1990s: International standardization 2000s: Continuous updates to regulations Important Events: Thalidomide scandal (1960s) - Led to the creation of GMP regulations Establishment of ICH (1990) - To internationalize standards Continuous updates to regulations Why is GMP Important?

> **Pharmaceutical Quality & Compliance Training Script**

## Overview

These three practices together form the quality framework in the pharmaceutical industry, but each focuses on a different stage.

GMP - Good Manufacturing Practice  GMP, as we learned, focuses on the manufacturing stage.

- **Scope**: Drug manufacturing Quality control during manufacturing Storage and distribution Facility and equipment management Objective: Ensure product quality consistency Prevent contamination and errors Ensure regulatory compliance Examples: Manufacturing tablets and capsules Filling liquid medications Storing raw materials and finished products GCP - Good Clinical Practice  GCP focuses on the clinical trial stage.
- **Scope**: Designing clinical trials Conducting clinical trials Collecting and analyzing data Protecting participants' rights Objective: Ensure participant safety in trials Collect reliable data Protect human rights Examples: Phase I trials Phase II/III trials Bioavailability studies GCP includes: Ethical approval Participant selection Trial monitoring Report preparation GLP - Good Laboratory Practice  GLP focuses on the preclinical research and development stage.
- **Scope**: Preclinical studies Toxicity testing Efficacy testing Sample analysis Objective: Ensure preclinical data quality Minimize laboratory errors Ensure result consistency Examples: Toxicity testing on animals Stability studies Efficacy testing in laboratory GLP includes: Laboratory management Employee training Equipment maintenance Document management Relationship Between the Three  GMP, GCP, and GLP complement each other.

> **Pharmaceutical Quality & Compliance Training Script**

## Overview

Cleaning and sterilization are essential to prevent contamination and ensure product quality.

Cleaning Principles  Cleaning is the removal of visible and invisible contaminants from equipment and facilities.

- **Types of Contaminants**: Chemical Contaminants: Residual chemicals Biological Contaminants: Bacteria and fungi Physical Contaminants: Dust and particles Residual Contaminants: Residues from previous products Cleaning Principles: Cleaning must be systematic Use appropriate cleaning agents Follow documented procedures Verify cleaning effectiveness Cleaning must be performed after each use Cleaning agents must be suitable for equipment Procedures must be documented in SOPs Cleaning Methods  There are several cleaning methods, and choosing the appropriate method depends on the equipment and product.
- **Main Methods**: Manual Cleaning: Using brushes and cleaning agents Automated Cleaning: Using CIP (Clean-in-Place) systems Foam Cleaning: Using foam for cleaning Ultrasonic Cleaning: For small and delicate parts for Each Method: Manual Cleaning: Suitable for equipment that cannot be cleaned automatically Requires employee training Must have clear procedures Automated Cleaning (CIP): Suitable for pipes and tanks More consistent and less error-prone Requires regular system maintenance Foam Cleaning: Suitable for large surfaces
- Reduces water usage Takes longer time Ultrasonic Cleaning: Suitable for small parts Effective in removing fine contaminants Requires careful monitoring Cleaning Agents  Selecting appropriate cleaning agents is essential for cleaning effectiveness.
- **Types of Cleaning Agents**: Alkaline Agents: To remove fats and oils Acidic Agents: To remove mineral deposits Solvent Agents: To remove organic contaminants Oxidizing Agents: For sterilization Alkaline agents such as caustic soda Acidic agents such as nitric acid Solvent agents such as ethanol Oxidizing agents such as hydrogen peroxide Considerations for Selecting Cleaning Agents: Effectiveness against contaminants Compatibility with equipment Ease of rinsing Safety for employees Cleaning Effectiveness Verification  Verifying cleaning effectiveness is essential to ensure no residual contaminants remain.
- **Verification Methods**: Visual Inspection: Verify no visible residues TOC Test: Total Organic Carbon Conductivity Test: To detect ionic residues pH Test: To detect acidic or alkaline residues Bacteria Test: To detect biological contamination Visual inspection is the first step TOC detects organic residues Conductivity detects ionic residues Bacteria test is essential for clean areas Sterilization  Sterilization is the removal or killing of all living microorganisms.
